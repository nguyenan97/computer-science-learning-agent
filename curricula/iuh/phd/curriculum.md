# IUH PhD in Computer Science — Condensed Curriculum

> **Source basis:** Industrial University of Ho Chi Minh City (IUH), *PhD Program in Computer Science*, program code `9480101`, curriculum version October 2022.
>
> This file is a learning/research-oriented Markdown extraction. It keeps the program objectives, outcomes, curriculum structure, doctoral courses and research components, while omitting lecturer contacts, signatures, repetitive assessment matrices, detailed administrative forms, long bibliography lists and other material that is not needed by the learning agent. It is **not** a substitute for current IUH admission, graduation or publication regulations.

## Program intent

The doctoral program is designed to develop researchers who can:

- Master deep, current and comprehensive Computer Science knowledge.
- Conduct independent and creative scientific research.
- Discover and analyze complex problems and propose valuable new solutions.
- Evaluate their own work and related research critically.
- Publish and present scientific results.
- Communicate academically in a foreign language.
- Lead and guide research teams.
- Work with professional responsibility and research ethics.

# Doctoral course summaries

## Deep Learning — `6201100`

**Purpose**

Develop a research-level understanding of deep-learning approaches, applications and limitations, and apply deep-learning methods to concrete problems such as Computer Vision and NLP.

**Core content in the detailed syllabus**

1. **Deep-learning fundamentals**
   - Overfitting / underfitting.
   - Data preprocessing, feature engineering and feature learning.
   - Neural networks.
   - Optimization techniques.
   - Regularization.
2. **Convolutional Neural Networks**
   - Convolution and pooling.
   - Variants and efficient convolution algorithms.
   - Pretrained models such as AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet and InceptionV3.
   - CNN applications in Computer Vision.
3. **Recurrent and recursive networks**
   - RNNs and computational graphs.
   - Bidirectional RNNs.
   - LSTM, GRU and Bi-LSTM.
   - Encoder-decoder / sequence-to-sequence architectures.
   - Deep recurrent networks.
   - NLP applications.
4. **Deep-learning research topics**
   - GANs.
   - Deep Q-Learning.
   - Deep generative models.

**Key outcomes**

- Analyze deep-learning problem-solving principles.
- Explain neural-network, CNN, RNN and reinforcement-learning approaches.
- Implement applications in Computer Vision, NLP and time-series forecasting.

> **Source inconsistency:** the curriculum structure/table assigns this course **4 credits**, while the detailed course-outline page states **3 total credits** and separately shows `2` theory + `2` practice. The repository retains the program-table value as the curriculum-level value and documents the discrepancy here.

## Big Data Analytics — `6201109`

**Purpose**

Analyze Big Data system architecture and design/implement data-processing components using the tools specified by the curriculum.

**Core content**

- Big Data system examples and architecture.
- Apache Pig and Pig Latin.
- Apache Hive and HiveQL.
- Spark SQL.
- Performance optimization with Pig, Hive and Spark SQL.
- Design and implementation of a simple Big Data processing component.
- Research/project reporting around a data-processing system.

**Key outcomes**

- Analyze Big Data architecture.
- Design a processing component using Pig/Hive/Spark SQL.
- Implement Big Data analysis with the covered tools.

## Modern Networking and Communication Technologies — `6201101`

**Purpose**

Strengthen networking foundations and analyze modern networking/communication trends with a research orientation.

**Core content**

- Computer-network fundamentals.
- Internet architecture.
- Next-generation networks.
- Communication technologies and wireless networks.
- Wireless sensor networks and design issues.
- IoT architecture.
- Cloud computing.
- Modern technologies identified in the curriculum such as SDN, NFV and QoE.
- Intelligent/smart networking and independent research reporting.

- Explain modern networking and communication technologies.
- Analyze technology trends.
- Read literature and communicate research findings independently.

## Advanced Computer Vision — `6201102`

**Prerequisite relationships in source:** Image Processing (prior), Computer Vision (prerequisite), Deep Learning (co-requisite).

**Purpose**

Study advanced Computer Vision techniques and apply them to image/video problems involving motion, human activities and multi-camera settings.

**Core content**

1. Image formation: geometry, photometry and segmentation.
2. Multi-view geometry.
3. Traditional object-recognition methods.
4. Deep-learning object recognition.
5. Face detection, face analysis and feature detection.
6. Image tracking, single/multiple cameras and feature correspondence.
7. Motion analysis and activity recognition.
8. Video processing.

**Key outcomes**

- Explain advanced image/video analysis techniques.
- Compare traditional and deep-learning Computer Vision approaches.
- Apply methods to face analysis, tracking, feature analysis and activity recognition.
- Solve practical advanced vision problems using image/video sequences.

**Purpose**

**Core content**

- Web and network fundamentals.
- Cryptography.
- Hacking techniques.
- Packet analysis and penetration testing.
- Social engineering.
- Cyber countermeasures.
- Incident response and mitigation.
- Digital forensics.
- Special topics including counter-surveillance and IoT security.

**Key outcomes**

- Analyze strengths and weaknesses of an information system.
- Propose improvements to system security.

## High Performance Computing — `6201104`

**Purpose**

Develop theoretical and practical understanding of high-performance/parallel computing, algorithm design and programming environments.

**Core content**

1. Parallel-processing overview and parallel computer architectures.
2. Parallel computation models and levels of parallelism.
3. Problem decomposition and mapping onto parallel architectures.
4. Parallel-performance analysis and **Amdahl's Law**.
5. Synchronization and critical sections.
6. Shared-memory programming and **OpenMP**.
7. Message-passing programming and **MPI**.
8. Parallel algorithms: matrix algorithms, embarrassingly parallel workloads, partitioning, divide-and-conquer.
9. **OpenCL** and **CUDA** programming.
10. Clusters and grid computing.
11. Large-scale graph analysis.
12. Computational aspects of neural networks / deep learning.

**Key outcomes**

- Explain parallel architectures and computation models.
- Analyze complexity/performance in parallel processing.
- Design and implement scientific applications with parallel programming environments.
- Understand current HPC application/technology trends.

---

## Parallel Programming for Multicore Systems — `6201105`

**Credits:** 4.

**Purpose**

Develop parallel-programming skills for contemporary multicore architectures.

**Core content**

- Parallel-programming fundamentals.
- Shared-memory programming with OpenMP.
- Distributed-memory programming with MPI.
- Hybrid computing and advanced OpenMP.
- CUDA parallel programming on multicore/GPU systems.
- The source overview also discusses multithreading, shared memory, data parallelism and higher-level parallel-programming models.

**Key outcomes**

- Explain the structure and use of parallel computers.
- Measure performance of parallel algorithms/computers.
- Develop programs for different parallel-computing models.

---

## Modeling and Simulation Techniques — `6201106`

**Purpose**

Model complex systems mathematically and simulate real-system behavior from those models.

**Core content**

1. System models and system simulation.
2. Model verification and validation.
3. Differential equations in simulation.
4. Discrete-system simulation, including queueing systems.
5. Continuous simulation.
6. Simulation languages, including SIMULA in the source syllabus.
7. Use of databases and AI in modeling and simulation.

**Key outcomes**

- Explain system modeling and model verification/validation.
- Apply discrete and continuous simulation techniques.
- Use modeling/simulation techniques on real systems, including database/AI-supported approaches.

# Research components

## Literature Review — `6201301` (2 credits)

The literature review is not treated as a simple reading summary. In the source it functions as a detailed dissertation proposal artifact.

**Expected content**

- Introduce the intended research area/problem.
- Analyze and evaluate recent domestic/international related work.
- Define the proposed dissertation topic.
- Define research tasks and expected results.
- Describe candidate methods.
- Connect existing/preliminary research results to the dissertation direction.

**Expected capabilities**

- Discover and analyze new research problems.
- Propose valuable solution ideas.
- State novelty/contribution to Computer Science.
- Evaluate a proposed solution.
- Move toward publication of research results.

---

## Research Topic 1 — `6201200` (3 credits)

Research Topic 1 requires the doctoral candidate to independently update knowledge directly related to the dissertation direction and report before an evaluation committee.

**Expected capabilities**

- Present an advanced/specialized Computer Science problem.
- Propose a solution and evaluate results.
- Write and present a research report.

For the learning agent, this component is best interpreted as **state-of-the-art study + bounded baseline/reproduction/initial experiment**.

---

## Research Topic 2 — `6201201` (3 credits)

Research Topic 2 has the same formal role of updating advanced knowledge related to the dissertation and solving a portion of the thesis problem, but occurs later in the sequence.

**Expected capabilities**

- Present an advanced research problem.
- Propose and evaluate a solution.
- Write and present research findings.

For the learning agent, this is naturally positioned after Research Topic 1 and can focus on a stronger extension, improved evaluation or contribution candidate.

---

## PhD Thesis — `6201999` (70 credits)

The thesis is the dominant research component of the program.

**Source requirements at the curriculum level**

- Literature Review and doctoral Research Topics are completed before thesis execution.
- Research must evaluate the current state of knowledge/technology related to the dissertation topic.
- Data collection, investigation or experimentation is performed where needed.
- Scientific reasoning or solution design is linked to experimentation/evidence.
- Results are analyzed and evaluated.
- The dissertation must be the candidate's own creative scientific work.
- It must make theoretical and/or practical contribution or introduce a valuable new solution.
- Results should be new and non-duplicative relative to prior work.
- Core research results are expected to be communicated through scientific publication/presentation according to applicable regulations.

**Key doctoral capabilities**

- Discover and analyze new problems.
- Propose valuable initiatives/solutions.
- Demonstrate novelty and contribution.
- Evaluate the proposed solution rigorously.
- Publish research results.
- Use a foreign language in research/publication.
- Guide research teams.
- Maintain professional responsibility, ethics and collaboration.
