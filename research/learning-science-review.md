# Learning science review and repository decisions

Checked: **2026-10-05**. Scope: daily graduate Computer Science study with unknown initial skill, bounded time, coding/analysis practice, delayed retention and transfer. This is a critical evidence synthesis with **incomplete primary-source access**, not a completed systematic review. No new experiments or effect sizes are claimed.

## Research method and verification boundary

Prioritize systematic reviews/meta-analyses, original experiments and academic syntheses. Candidate literature was selected around retrieval, spacing, interleaving, explanation, examples, feedback, cognitive load, productive failure, mastery and transfer. This is purposive selection, not an exhaustive search; no preregistered search, double screening, risk-of-bias rating or publication-bias analysis was performed.

The proxy refused DOI requests (403 tunnel errors). We did not bypass that restriction. We directly read **Deans for Impact (2015), The Science of Learning**, including its reference list, and The Carpentries instructor-training episodes on practice, memory, feedback and teaching practices. These are reputable **secondary instructional syntheses**, not substitutes for independently examining a meta-analysis's inclusion criteria, effect heterogeneity or an experiment's methods. The Carpentries audience is usually novice computational learners, not graduate CS students. Generalization to daily agent tutoring remains a design hypothesis.

Read PDF: [pinned accessible copy](https://github.com/carpentries/instructor-training/blob/50745001271700a108de0622d80341965e249e5b/episodes/files/papers/science-of-learning-2015.pdf). Read teaching material: [memory and cognitive load](https://github.com/carpentries/instructor-training/blob/50745001271700a108de0622d80341965e249e5b/episodes/05-memory.md), [practice and formative assessment](https://github.com/carpentries/instructor-training/blob/50745001271700a108de0622d80341965e249e5b/episodes/02-practice-learning.md), [feedback](https://github.com/carpentries/instructor-training/blob/50745001271700a108de0622d80341965e249e5b/episodes/06-feedback.md). The PDF questions 1–4 connect prerequisites, worked examples, spaced testing, interleaving, task-focused feedback and comparisons of underlying structures to cited research. Some Carpentries wording is stronger than warranted (for example “teaching is one of the most effective ways to learn”); this repo does not adopt a universal ranking.

[Machine-readable access log](source-checks.json) records URLs, date/time, availability and content hashes for successfully downloaded documents. For unavailable primary papers below, links and bibliographic details are **candidate citations**, not claims of full-text verification. Check them through institutional access before claiming precise effects or study-specific conclusions. In particular, productive-failure and mastery-learning recommendations below are provisional design guidance; their selected meta-analyses could not be examined here.

## Evidence calibration

**Broadly supported principle** means convergent established literature and agreement with the accessible syntheses; it does not certify every task, student or effect size. **Context-dependent** means task design, prior knowledge, feedback or comparison conditions can change the result. **Design heuristic** means a reversible repo choice to evaluate using learner evidence.

| Method / confidence | Suitable knowledge or skill | Apply / limit | Concrete daily activity | Measure effectiveness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice — broadly supported for durable recall; transfer conditional | Definitions, invariants, concept distinctions, reconstructing reasoning and procedures | Ask for an attempt without notes; give corrective feedback. Avoid making every retrieval high stakes, cueing the answer, or repeatedly retrieving a misconception without correction. Novices may need initial teaching first. | Reconstruct a loop invariant, predict a query result, select an algorithm and justify it. Then inspect a test and repair the reasoning. | Save unaided response, hints and errors; test a different prompt later, record actual delay and accuracy separately from same-day success. |
| Spaced practice — broadly supported for retention, interval choice conditional | Knowledge/procedures that must remain accessible over weeks; recurring error patterns | Distribute retrieval/practice across sessions. Desired retention horizon, prior knowledge and task matter. Do not claim one universal optimal schedule or impose a large backlog on a short session. | Create a review prompt after an observed attempt/completion, not when generating a lesson. Move next due date after actual retrieval evidence with a recorded reason. | Delayed success, actual elapsed days, hint use and overdue exposure; compare trends for similar task difficulty, not raw streak length. |
| Interleaving — context-dependent | Discriminating competing strategies: BFS/Dijkstra, seek/scan, lower/upper bounds | Begin with enough blocked practice to form a model; then mix related alternatives. Arbitrarily rotating unrelated courses increases switching cost and does not ensure discrimination. | Present 2–3 cases and ask which strategy fits before solving; explain the discriminating feature. | Strategy selection and justification on unseen cases after delay; score separately from implementation accuracy. |
| Self-explanation — context-dependent with substantial supporting literature | Causal reasoning, proofs, worked-example decisions, debugging | Use specific “why this step?” prompts; compare with evidence. Fluent but incorrect explanations need correction. Limit prompts if they overwhelm a novice. | Explain why `lo = mid + 1` preserves the partition; state a counterexample to an alternative. | Explanation rubric: mechanism, assumptions, counterexample and trade-off; later explain a changed boundary/task without looking. |
| Elaboration — context-dependent; background knowledge required | Linking new concepts to known structures and meaningful examples | Use constrained analogies and meaningful “how/why” questions. Unchecked analogies or ornamental stories can reinforce errors. | Connect array search partitioning to an ordered time-window query; identify where the analogy stops. | Correct connection and explicit boundary of analogy; apply it to a new domain rather than repeating the story. |
| Worked examples, scaffolding, faded guidance — broadly supported for novices; expertise reversal conditional | Multi-step procedures, invariants, mathematical derivations, unfamiliar tools | Narrate decisions with one compact example, then remove steps as evidence improves. Experts may benefit more from an independent problem; avoid persistent redundant guidance. | Worked trace → partially completed implementation → independent range query → novel record/key variation. | Reduce hints while maintaining correctness on new inputs; independent transfer and later reconstruction, not copied code. |
| Deliberate practice — context-dependent; definition matters | Specific subskills with clear performance standards and corrective feedback | Target a diagnosed weakness and repeat a purposeful variation. Daily coding alone is not “deliberate practice”; evidence from music/sport does not establish a CS mastery clock. | Diagnose off-by-one error, construct a minimal counterexample, fix it and solve a changed boundary case. | Error recurrence, quality of debugging explanation, independence and delayed reproduction; do not infer skill solely from hours. |
| Feedback / misconception repair — broadly supported purpose, timing/content conditional | Conceptual misunderstandings and procedural/tool errors | Give specific task-focused feedback after an attempt; prompt correction and reattempt. Immediate hints can spoil useful retrieval; delaying essential setup feedback can waste time. | “Your rule skips the first duplicate; trace `[2,2]` at x=2.” Ask learner to revise the invariant and rerun a new case. | Save original error, feedback, corrected explanation and reattempt; revisit the misconception after delay. Test success alone may hide a wrong model. |
| Cognitive load — established framework; specific intervention context-dependent | Novices juggling new concepts, tools and multi-step reasoning | Reduce irrelevant setup; integrate explanations with the relevant code; chunk steps. Do not treat difficulty or self-reported effort as a direct memory-capacity measure. | Offline deterministic starter; one invariant; checkpoints; optional tooling rather than Docker/cloud for a simple algorithm. | Completion time and tool friction separately from conceptual errors, reported effort and delayed learning; lower effort alone is not improvement. |
| Productive failure — context-dependent, provisional here | Prepared learners exploring a generative problem before explicit consolidation | Use a brief bounded attempt followed by contrasting solutions and teaching. Avoid unguided struggle with weak prerequisites or no debrief; failure itself is not evidence of learning. | Predict duplicate-search behavior, try a candidate rule, compare a counterexample and the worked trace. Offer direct example first to novices. | Quality of alternatives and subsequent independent transfer versus similar taught tasks; never award mastery for struggling. |
| Transfer of learning — context-dependent; broad transfer hard | Applying underlying structure to changed data, constraints or representations | Teach multiple cases and explicitly compare structures. One coding task cannot establish general engineering expertise. | Reuse boundary reasoning for timestamp windows, then distinguish arrays from database B-trees; discuss update costs. | Separate near transfer (different inputs) from changed-context transfer (records/range query); assess again later without hints. |
| Mastery learning / difficulty adaptation — context-dependent, provisional here | Bounded objectives with diagnostic assessment, corrective instruction and reassessment | Establish objective-specific success criteria before practice; adjust scaffolding to error evidence. Do not assume a universal 65/85% gate or equate completion with mastery. | Low result → misconception-specific remediation; independent same-day result → provisional; delayed recall + changed-context performance → stronger scoped evidence. | Multiple independent observations and actual delays, rubric and task context. Store uncertainty; challenge difficulty and hints must accompany any numeric score. |

## Concrete repository recommendations and measurement

1. Replace generation-as-progress with a lifecycle and evidence-bearing assessment records. Preserve null score/explanation when unknown. This addresses the most consequential pre-change failure mode.
2. Default to one core objective, but permit review-only and prerequisite repair. Allocate by the learner's time and tool constraints; short/standard/extended are product heuristics, not scientific time ratios.
3. Give a worked example, guided practice, independent task and changed-context task. Reveal hints progressively and keep challenge solutions in separate mentor material.
4. Keep completion separate from a conservative, objective-scoped evidence summary. Same-day independent success is provisional. Delayed retrieval and transfer increase confidence; one day is only the minimum observable separation for a date-granularity schema, **not** a scientifically sufficient retention interval.
5. Schedule a next review only after an observed attempt/completion, and retain each actual review attempt and schedule decision. +1/+3/+7/+21 or doubling intervals are optional heuristics, never a finding. Failed recall calls for explanation/correction and an earlier check selected with the learner; success may justify a longer interval within their goal horizon.
6. Evaluate local effectiveness over weeks: delayed task performance, hint reliance, repeated misconceptions, explanation quality and transfer, with task difficulty and actual delays recorded. Do not call a before/after trend causal; workload, familiarity and topic difficulty confound it.
7. Treat GitHub stars as discovery metadata, not an outcome or quality score. Pin the smallest relevant implementation/test slice, inspect its behavior and record what was actually verified.
